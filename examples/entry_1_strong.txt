Why do cats purr? Cats purr by rapidly twitching the muscles of the larynx, which makes the vocal folds vibrate as air passes over them, about 25 times per second. Purring is not only a sign of contentment. Cats also purr when stressed, injured or giving birth, and researchers think the low-frequency vibration may help soothe the cat and even support bone and tissue healing. A concrete example: a kitten purrs while nursing, which tells its mother it is safe and fed. So a purr is best understood as a self-soothing and communication tool, not just a happy noise.
